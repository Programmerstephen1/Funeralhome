# Ethical backlink and local authority plan

Backlinks require real third-party publication and cannot be manufactured by website code. Use relevant, editorially earned links and avoid paid link schemes, automated directories, reciprocal link farms, or keyword-stuffed guest posts.

## First 30 days

1. Verify the final public domain, business name, phone, Kahawa West address/service area, and operating hours with the business owner.
2. Claim and complete the Google Business Profile and Bing Places listing. Keep name, address, phone, website, and hours consistent with the site.
3. Submit the sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools after DNS and HTTPS are stable.
4. Request accurate citations from legitimate Kenyan business and funeral-service directories; review each publisher before submitting.
5. Ask partner organizations that actually work with the business (venues, transport providers, florists, faith/community organizations) to link to a useful service page where relevant.

## Ongoing editorial work

- Publish useful, locally relevant planning guides reviewed by the funeral-care team; cite official legal and public-service sources.
- Offer a short downloadable planning checklist or service-area guide that community organizations can reference.
- Ask satisfied customers for feedback without conditioning service or incentives on a link or review.
- Track referring domains, qualified referral visits, unlinked business mentions, and spam links monthly. Contact publishers only for factual corrections or legitimate attribution.
- Do not disavow links routinely; investigate a specific manual action or clear manipulative-link attack before taking that step.

## Governance

Record each publisher, URL, relationship, date, and factual verification. Review links quarterly for ownership changes, dead destinations, inaccurate details, and privacy concerns. Never publish family or memorial information to obtain links.
